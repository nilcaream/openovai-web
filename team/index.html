The Leader and the Workers

Three actors. The User steers the team through the Leader, in plain words. The Leader hires, delegates, relays, settles what the instance may do and writes the hard rules; it does no project work. Workers do the work, one task each, and stop when it ships.

Three actors

User
You. The Leader's panel is in the middle; type what you want and the Leader starts. You can type on any Worker's panel too, and the Leader hears it the same moment. Rules about what the instance may do are yours, settled in your words on a card.
Leader
One session, always there. It takes your words, hires Workers by name, briefs them, takes their questions and reports, decides what reaches you, files a finished desk away and parks the room when you say the day is over. When it is not running, the panel shows its history, and the next thing addressed to it — a line you type, a Worker's message, a server event — starts it on its desk.
Worker
A named session on a desk of its own, with one task. It keeps its desk written as the work moves, reports to the Leader with a message, and stops when the task ships. A Worker's panel lives with its process; hired again on the same desk it is back with its rows.

The day of a Leader

You open the page and type on the Leader's panel: what you want, in any words. The Leader answers in plain language, and when the work is somebody's, it hires. Called with no name, the roster names the Worker: a fixed list of thirty first names, and the next one is the least recently used free one — never used first, then the one whose holder left longest ago. A new name gets a desk opened from the template, and the rules that make that directory the Worker's to write in. A name is for somebody who has a desk — somebody who stopped — started again on it, panel kept.

Then the Leader briefs: a message to the Worker, which comes back the moment the Worker has it, and the Leader's turn goes on. What the Worker says back arrives later, as a message the other way, and the Leader decides what of it reaches you. While the Leader's turn runs, one line above its panel reads what it is at — thinking, or the call it has out.

Through the day the server tells the Leader what it needs to know and nothing more: that you typed on a Worker's panel, and what; that one Worker said something to another; that a Worker has been silent for 10 minutes, and again at 50; that a Worker has been waiting on a card for ten minutes; that a Worker stopped idle, with its desk as it was last written; that a hard rule changed. Told, not asked: nothing waits on the Leader for any of it.

The rules the instance holds are settled through the Leader, in your words. Say "you will be autonomous, push without asking" or "pip install is too much, ask me every time", and the Leader answers in plain language first, then calls permission once per rule — a card on its panel with Allow, Deny and Ask every time. A Worker hired to push is hired after the push is allowed. Permissions has the whole of it.

When a Worker's round is done the Leader retires it: desks/<Name>/ is moved whole — the desk, the conversation, the persona — under archive/, the seat leaves the room and the page, and the name is the roster's again. Filed, never deleted. One that may be wanted back on the same desk is stopped, not retired. And when you say this is it for the day, in any words, the Leader parks the room: every Worker writes its desk and stops.

What a Worker does

One task at a time. A Worker is started on its desk with a persona rendered at that moment, reads its desk first, and works from there: the desk directory is its working directory, so what the work produces — a design, a finding, notes — is kept beside the desk file. It writes the desk with a tool at every point the work moves, not only at the end, because a written desk is what survives and anything worked out and not written is gone with the session.

A Worker reaches the Leader with message, and only that way: what it says on its own panel lands there and nowhere else. Seven tools go to every seat; the four that are the Leader's — park, hire, retire, permission — refuse a Worker in words, so a Worker that wants a rule proposes it to the Leader. When the task ships the Worker reports, writes its desk and stops with stop_session; the desk stays, and hire on its name brings it back on the same desk if it is wanted again.

Talking

Between sessions there is one way to talk: message, to a named seat. It is refused when the other seat has no process; a seat held by the quota gate has it and reads it when the window resets. A message between the Leader and a Worker is one line on the Leader's panel and a full row on the Worker's; a message from one Worker to another reaches both, and lands on the Leader's panel too as Paul → Jane: … — heard, not asked. A message whose markdown opens with a table, a list or a code block folds behind one line; a double click opens it.

When you type on a Worker's panel, the words are that Worker's own <user> turn — your authority, for a typed "go" — and the server tells the Leader the same moment: one line on the Leader's panel, Ana → Paul: …, and an event to its process. The Leader hears what you typed as you typed it, and the Worker does not have to pass it on.

Every one of these arrives in a frame the server set — <user>, <message from="Max">, <server-event> — so a session always knows who is speaking, and nothing inside a message can pose as somebody else.

Desks and handover

A desk is a person. The desk file is written with write_desk — a title, one line, what the seat is on; a status, one line, where it stands; and the body, the sections — and the server writes the header line. It is how the User and the Leader see what everybody is on without opening every panel.

When a session's conversation passes the ceiling, the server sends it one event, context-full. The session writes its desk, tells the Leader in one line that it is restarting and where the work stands, calls restart_session, and says "back in a moment". A successor starts on the same desk, in the same panel, with whatever was queued for it carried over, and reads the desk first. Both restart_session and stop_session refuse until the desk was written since the event that asked, so the desk comes first, always.

Models per seat

The instance was installed with a model for the Leader and one for the Workers. A Worker that should not run on the usual one is hired with a model beside its name, kept as one word in desks/<Name>/MODEL — its own file rather than a line of the desk, because the desk file is written by its session, and a model kept in there would be a model that session could raise for itself. It goes away with the desk. Leaving the model out writes nothing down, so changing the installed model moves everybody who was never named one, from their next process. ovai configuration lists every desk with what it resolves to.

Idle and quota, from the team's side

A Worker that has been silent is reported to the Leader at 10 and at 50 minutes. At 55 the seat itself is told it goes cold at 60, and writes its desk and stops. A seat on a turn — waiting on a card included — is never idle. What is not stopped by 60 is ended with its desk as it was last written, and the Leader is told.

When a usage window nears its limit every running seat is told: a Worker writes its desk and stops; the Leader tells you which window and when it resets, writes its desk and stays. No Worker is hired until the reset. A Worker still mid-turn when the window is spent is interrupted and told again — do not resume, write your desk and stop. After the reset the Leader brings the Workers back with hire on their desks. The quota gate has the stages and the thresholds.
